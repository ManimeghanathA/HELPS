Terminology and Definitions
This section defines the key terms used throughout this System Design Document (SDD). These definitions establish a common understanding of the concepts, entities, and components that form the foundation of the Helicopter Emergency Landing Place Suggestion System (HELPSs). Unless otherwise specified, the following terminology shall be used consistently throughout the remainder of this document.

Area of Interest (AOI): A geographically bounded region selected for terrain analysis and knowledge generation. All satellite imagery, Digital Elevation Models (DEM), and auxiliary geospatial datasets processed by HELPSs correspond to the selected Area of Interest.

Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline: The backend processing pipeline responsible for collecting geospatial data, identifying candidate terrain, extracting terrain characteristics, generating operational zones, and constructing the Terrain Knowledge Database. This pipeline is executed whenever a new geographical region is analysed or when existing terrain information requires updating.

Helicopter Landing Recommendation Pipeline: The mission-time decision support pipeline responsible for retrieving candidate terrain from the Terrain Knowledge Database, evaluating it against helicopter-specific operational requirements, and generating ranked emergency landing recommendations.

Terrain Knowledge Database: The centralized spatial database generated by the Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline. It stores all helicopter-independent terrain information, including terrain objects, operational zones, terrain characteristics, environmental attributes, and spatial relationships required during recommendation generation.

Terrain Object: A contiguous region of candidate terrain identified after eliminating permanent obstacles such as buildings, dense vegetation, steep cliffs, water bodies, or other unsuitable regions. A Terrain Object represents a physically continuous area that is considered suitable for further analysis but is not yet guaranteed to satisfy helicopter landing requirements.

Candidate Terrain: Terrain that satisfies the minimum criteria required for further analysis following obstacle removal. Candidate Terrain serves as the initial input to the terrain characterization and operational zone generation processes.

Terrain Characterization: The process of extracting descriptive information from each Terrain Object. This includes geometric properties, terrain composition, surface characteristics, vegetation density, accessibility, surrounding infrastructure, and other helicopter-independent attributes required for subsequent recommendation generation.

Operational Zone: A contiguous region within a Terrain Object that satisfies a specified terrain slope threshold. Multiple Operational Zones may exist within a single Terrain Object, each representing a different slope constraint to accommodate helicopters with varying operational limitations.
Slope Profile: The complete collection of Operational Zones generated for a Terrain Object across multiple slope thresholds. Instead of representing terrain using a single slope value, HELPSs preserves a multi-threshold slope representation to support helicopters with different permissible landing slopes.

Representative Point: A reference point located within an Operational Zone that is maximally distant from its surrounding boundaries. HELPSs utilizes the Pole of Inaccessibility as the Representative Point because it provides the safest and most centrally located position for evaluating landing feasibility and clearance.

Pole of Inaccessibility: The point inside a polygon that has the maximum possible distance from all polygon boundaries. This point represents the center of the largest empty circle that can be inscribed within the polygon and is used by HELPSs as the reference location for landing footprint validation.

Landing Footprint: The physical area required for a helicopter to safely occupy an Operational Zone, including the rotor diameter and the necessary operational safety clearance. Landing Footprint validation is performed during the Helicopter Landing Recommendation Pipeline and depends on helicopter-specific operational characteristics.

Environmental Context: The collection of environmental attributes surrounding an Operational Zone, including accessibility, nearby infrastructure, surrounding obstacles, vegetation, water bodies, road connectivity, terrain openness, and other operationally relevant information used during recommendation ranking.

Approach Corridor: The surrounding airspace through which a helicopter approaches or departs an Operational Zone. In Version 1 of HELPSs, the suitability of the approach corridor is represented using an Approach Corridor Score derived from surrounding terrain openness and nearby obstacle information.

Approach Corridor Score: A numerical measure representing the relative openness and accessibility of an Operational Zone based on its surrounding environment. The score provides an estimate of how favorable the surrounding terrain is for helicopter approach and departure operations and serves as one of the factors used during recommendation ranking.

Mission Context: The operational objective associated with a helicopter mission. Examples include emergency engine failure, fuel emergency, medical evacuation, disaster response, and search-and-rescue operations. Mission Context influences the weighting of ranking parameters during recommendation generation.

Recommendation Score: A composite suitability score assigned to every candidate Operational Zone after evaluating helicopter compatibility, environmental conditions, landing feasibility, accessibility, mission context, and other operational constraints. This score is used to rank the final emergency landing recommendations presented to the pilot.
Chapter 1: Problem Understanding

Background
Helicopters play a critical role in search and rescue, emergency medical services, disaster response, military operations, border surveillance, and offshore logistics due to their ability to take off and land vertically, hover, and operate in environments inaccessible to conventional fixed-wing aircraft. These missions frequently require helicopters to operate over dense forests, mountainous terrain, deserts, coastal regions, and other remote areas, where dedicated landing infrastructure is often unavailable.
During emergency situations such as engine failures, mechanical malfunctions, fuel emergencies, or adverse weather conditions, pilots must rapidly identify a safe landing location while simultaneously maintaining control of the aircraft. This decision requires evaluating multiple terrain characteristics, including terrain slope, surface conditions, surrounding obstacles, available landing clearance, and accessibility, all within a limited time frame and under significant operational stress.
The increasing availability of high-resolution satellite imagery, Digital Elevation Models (DEM), Geographic Information System (GIS) data, and geospatial analysis techniques has made large-scale terrain understanding feasible. These technologies provide the foundation for developing intelligent decision-support systems capable of analyzing terrain, identifying potential emergency landing opportunities, and assisting pilots with objective, data-driven recommendations, while preserving the pilot's role as the final decision-maker
Problem Statement

Helicopter emergency landing is a time-critical decision-making problem where pilots must rapidly identify a safe and operationally feasible landing location while simultaneously managing the aircraft under emergency conditions. Unlike fixed-wing aircraft, helicopters frequently operate in remote and unprepared environments where dedicated landing infrastructure is unavailable.
Identifying a suitable landing location involves evaluating multiple terrain characteristics, including terrain slope, surface type, surrounding obstacles, vegetation density, water bodies, landing clearance, and accessibility. Performing this assessment manually during an emergency significantly increases the pilot's cognitive workload and the likelihood of overlooking suitable landing opportunities.
Although modern navigation systems provide positioning, mapping, and terrain visualization, they do not automatically evaluate terrain suitability for helicopter emergency landings. The responsibility of interpreting the surrounding terrain and selecting a landing location remains entirely with the pilot.
Additionally, different helicopters have different operational constraints, such as rotor diameter, landing footprint, allowable slope, and surface limitations. Building separate terrain databases for every helicopter type is computationally inefficient, difficult to maintain, and not scalable.
To address this challenge, HELPSs proposes a helicopter-independent terrain knowledge framework that transforms raw geospatial data into structured terrain information. Instead of directly searching for landing locations for a specific helicopter, the system first understands and characterizes the terrain, and later evaluates it using helicopter-specific operational requirements during mission execution.
This separation enables a scalable, reusable, and explainable decision-support system that assists pilots by reducing search time, providing objective terrain analysis, and presenting ranked landing recommendations, while ensuring that the pilot remains the final decision-maker.
1.3 Challenges in Helicopter Emergency Landing
Emergency helicopter landing is a high-pressure, time-critical operation in which pilots must simultaneously control the aircraft while identifying a safe landing location. Unlike planned landings, emergency situations provide limited decision time, often requiring rapid assessment of unfamiliar terrain under stressful conditions.
The complexity of this process arises from the need to evaluate multiple terrain characteristics simultaneously. A seemingly open area may contain steep slopes, dense vegetation, water bodies, power lines, surrounding obstacles, or insufficient landing clearance, making it unsuitable for helicopter operations. Furthermore, different helicopters have different operational limitations, requiring the landing location to satisfy aircraft-specific constraints such as allowable slope, landing footprint, and safety clearance.
The challenge therefore extends beyond locating an open area. It requires rapid identification, evaluation, and comparison of multiple terrain regions while ensuring that the selected location satisfies both terrain safety and helicopter operational requirements under severe time constraints.
1.4 Limitations of Existing Navigation and Landing Systems
Modern aviation navigation systems provide accurate positioning, route planning, terrain visualization, and obstacle awareness, significantly improving flight safety. However, these systems are not specifically designed to recommend emergency landing locations for helicopters.
Existing systems primarily present geographical information, leaving the pilot responsible for interpreting terrain characteristics and determining whether a location is suitable for landing. They do not automatically analyse terrain slope, landing clearance, surface conditions, surrounding obstacles, environmental accessibility, and helicopter-specific operational constraints as a unified decision-support process.
Additionally, most available systems are designed for navigation rather than terrain understanding. They do not construct a reusable knowledge base of terrain that can be evaluated for different helicopter platforms and mission requirements. Consequently, pilots must continue to perform complex terrain assessment manually during emergency situations, increasing cognitive workload when rapid decision-making is most critical.
1.5 Importance of Terrain Understanding
The suitability of an emergency landing location is determined by the characteristics of the terrain rather than its visual appearance. An open area may still be unsuitable due to steep slopes, dense vegetation, surrounding obstacles, poor surface conditions, or insufficient landing clearance. Therefore, identifying a safe landing location requires a comprehensive understanding of the terrain rather than simply locating empty space.
Terrain understanding involves analyzing and characterizing geographical regions using information such as terrain geometry, slope, surface type, vegetation, water bodies, environmental context, and accessibility. By transforming raw geospatial data into structured terrain knowledge, potential landing locations can be systematically identified and evaluated before being assessed against helicopter-specific operational requirements.
A terrain-centric approach enables the creation of a reusable and helicopter-independent knowledge base, allowing the same terrain information to support multiple helicopter platforms and mission scenarios. This significantly reduces repeated computation while providing a scalable foundation for intelligent emergency landing recommendation systems.
1.6 Project Objectives
The primary objective of HELPSs (Helicopter Emergency Landing Place Suggestion System) is to develop an AI-assisted terrain knowledge and decision-support system capable of identifying, characterizing, evaluating, and recommending potential emergency landing locations for helicopters.
The specific objectives of the proposed system are:
Develop a Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline capable of analyzing satellite imagery, Digital Elevation Models (DEM), and GIS datasets to identify candidate terrain. 
Generate a helicopter-independent terrain knowledge database by extracting geometric, environmental, and operational characteristics of each terrain object. 
Create operational zones based on multi-threshold terrain slope analysis while preserving terrain information for different helicopter capabilities. 
Develop a Helicopter Landing Recommendation Pipeline that evaluates terrain knowledge using helicopter-specific operational constraints such as landing footprint, allowable slope, and mission requirements. 
Provide explainable and ranked landing recommendations supported by terrain characteristics, environmental analysis, and operational evidence rather than blind suggestions. 
Assist pilots in reducing decision time and cognitive workload during emergency situations while ensuring that the pilot remains the final decision-maker.

1.7 Project Scope
The scope of HELPSs Version 1 is limited to the development of a terrain knowledge generation and helicopter landing recommendation system for emergency landing scenarios using publicly available geospatial data.
The system focuses on:
Generating terrain knowledge from satellite imagery, Digital Elevation Models (DEM), and GIS datasets. 
Identifying candidate terrain by eliminating regions containing permanent obstacles. 
Characterizing terrain using geometric, surface, environmental, and accessibility features. 
Generating operational zones through multi-threshold slope analysis. 
Constructing a reusable, helicopter-independent Terrain Knowledge Database. 
Evaluating terrain using helicopter-specific operational constraints to produce ranked emergency landing recommendations. 
Providing an offline, locally deployable decision-support system that preserves organizational confidentiality without relying on cloud-based processing. 
HELPSs excludes predefined restricted areas, such as military installations, airport runways, border-sensitive regions, government-protected facilities, and other legally prohibited zones, from terrain analysis and recommendation generation.
The current scope does not include autonomous flight control, automatic landing, real-time satellite imagery acquisition, or direct integration with onboard avionics.
1.8 Assumptions
The proposed system is developed based on the following assumptions:
Satellite imagery, DEM, and GIS datasets are available for the selected Area of Interest (AOI). 
Terrain conditions are assumed to remain relatively static between successive Terrain Knowledge Generation cycles. 
Helicopter operational specifications, such as rotor diameter, landing footprint, allowable slope, and safety clearance, are available during the recommendation phase. 
Weather and wind information can be obtained from reliable external sources when generating recommendations. 
The system operates as a decision-support tool, and the pilot retains full authority over the final landing decision. 
Publicly available geospatial datasets provide sufficient resolution for identifying candidate terrain suitable for Version 1. 

1.9 Limitations
Although HELPSs provides a structured framework for terrain understanding and emergency landing recommendation, Version 1 has several limitations.
The system primarily relies on publicly available geospatial datasets, whose spatial resolution and update frequency may limit terrain accuracy. Dynamic environmental conditions such as temporary obstacles, moving vehicles, wildlife, recent construction, or vegetation changes are not considered unless reflected in updated datasets.
The current implementation provides an approximate approach corridor assessment based on surrounding terrain openness and obstacle information rather than detailed helicopter flight dynamics. Similarly, weather and wind conditions are incorporated during the recommendation phase but are not integrated into terrain knowledge generation.
HELPSs is designed as a decision-support system and does not replace pilot judgment. The recommendations generated by the system are intended to assist situational awareness and reduce decision-making time, while the pilot remains responsible for evaluating operational safety and selecting the final landing location.
1.10 Chapter Summary
The need for an intelligent emergency landing assistance system arises from the time-critical nature of helicopter emergency operations and the complexity of evaluating terrain under high cognitive workload. Existing navigation systems primarily provide situational awareness but do not perform comprehensive terrain suitability analysis for emergency landings.
A safe emergency landing depends on multiple terrain characteristics, including geometry, slope, surface conditions, surrounding obstacles, environmental accessibility, and helicopter-specific operational constraints. This highlights the importance of transforming raw geospatial data into structured terrain knowledge that can be systematically evaluated during mission execution.
HELPSs addresses this need by adopting a terrain-centric, helicopter-independent, and explainable decision-support approach, establishing the foundation for the system architecture and design principles presented in the following chapters.




Chapter 2: Design Philosophy

2.1 Vision of HELPSs

The vision of HELPSs (Helicopter Emergency Landing Place Suggestion System) is to develop an AI-assisted terrain knowledge and decision-support system that enables rapid identification and evaluation of potential emergency landing locations for helicopters operating in challenging environments.

Rather than directly searching for landing sites, HELPSs aims to understand and characterize the terrain by transforming raw geospatial data into structured terrain knowledge. This knowledge can then be evaluated for different helicopter platforms based on their operational requirements, enabling the same terrain information to support multiple aircraft without repeated analysis.

HELPSs is designed as a decision-support system, not an autonomous decision-making system. The objective is to reduce the pilot's cognitive workload by narrowing the search space and presenting ranked, explainable, and evidence-based landing recommendations, while ensuring that the pilot remains the final authority in selecting the landing location.
The long-term vision of HELPSs is to establish a scalable and reusable terrain intelligence framework that can be extended to different geographical regions, integrated with higher-resolution datasets, and adapted for future operational requirements without fundamentally changing the system architecture.

2.2 Design Principles

The architecture of HELPSs is guided by a set of fundamental design principles that ensure the system remains modular, scalable, explainable, and adaptable.
Terrain-First Architecture: The system first develops a comprehensive understanding of the terrain before considering helicopter-specific operational constraints. This separation prevents repeated terrain analysis for different helicopter platforms. 
Helicopter-Independent Knowledge Generation: All terrain analysis is performed independently of any specific helicopter. Helicopter characteristics are considered only during the recommendation phase, allowing a single Terrain Knowledge Database to support multiple aircraft types. 
Progressive Terrain Characterization: Instead of immediately rejecting terrain based on helicopter limitations, HELPSs progressively extracts and stores terrain characteristics such as geometry, slope, surface composition, environmental context, and accessibility. This preserves valuable information for future evaluation. 
Explainable Recommendations: Every recommendation generated by HELPSs is accompanied by supporting evidence, including terrain characteristics, operational constraints, suitability scores, and environmental information. The system provides transparent reasoning rather than blind recommendations. 
Pilot-Centric Decision Support: HELPSs is designed to assist pilots, not replace them. The system provides objective terrain analysis while ensuring that the final operational decision always remains with the pilot. 
Confidential and Local Deployment: The recommendation engine is intended to operate entirely within a local computing environment, allowing organizations to perform mission planning and emergency landing analysis without transmitting sensitive operational data to external cloud services. 
Scalable and Modular Design: Each functional component of HELPSs is developed as an independent module with clearly defined inputs and outputs. This modular architecture simplifies maintenance, supports future enhancements, and enables integration of additional datasets or analytical capabilities without redesigning the complete system. 
HELPSs generates recommendations only from terrain that satisfies operational and regulatory constraints. Regions designated as restricted or prohibited for helicopter landing are excluded from analysis before terrain knowledge generation.

2.3 Terrain-First Philosophy

HELPSs adopts a terrain-first approach, where the system first analyzes and understands the terrain before considering any helicopter-specific constraints. By separating terrain understanding from helicopter evaluation, the same terrain knowledge can be reused for multiple helicopter platforms without repeated analysis, making the system scalable and efficient.

2.4 Helicopter-Independent Terrain Knowledge

Instead of generating landing locations for individual helicopters, HELPSs constructs a helicopter-independent Terrain Knowledge Database containing terrain geometry, surface characteristics, operational zones, and environmental information. During recommendation, this knowledge is evaluated using the operational constraints of the selected helicopter, enabling a single database to support multiple aircraft types.

2.5 Pilot-Centric Decision Support

HELPSs is designed to assist, not replace, the pilot. The system provides ranked and explainable landing recommendations supported by terrain analysis and operational evidence, while ensuring that the pilot remains the final decision-maker in all emergency landing scenarios.

2.6 Explainable Recommendation Strategy

HELPSs provides explainable recommendations rather than blind suggestions. Every recommended landing location is accompanied by supporting information such as distance to the location, terrain characteristics, slope, surface type, accessibility, environmental context, and suitability score, enabling pilots to understand why a location has been recommended.



2.7 Confidentiality and Local Processing

HELPSs is designed to operate as a locally deployable decision-support system, ensuring that sensitive mission information, helicopter specifications, and operational data remain within the organization's secure environment. Terrain knowledge is generated locally, and all recommendation computations are performed without relying on external cloud services, thereby preserving confidentiality, security, and operational integrity.

2.8 Adaptability to Different Geographic Regions

HELPSs is designed as a geographically adaptable framework rather than a region-specific solution. By utilizing satellite imagery, Digital Elevation Models (DEM), and GIS datasets, the system can generate terrain knowledge for any Area of Interest (AOI). This enables the same architecture to be deployed across different terrains and geographical regions with minimal modifications, making the system scalable for future operational requirements.
 
2.9 Scalability Towards Real-Time Deployment

HELPSs is designed with a modular and scalable architecture that supports future enhancements without requiring significant redesign. Although Version 1 generates terrain knowledge using periodically updated geospatial datasets, the same architecture can be extended to incorporate higher-resolution imagery, frequently updated terrain information, and real-time environmental data. This design enables HELPSs to evolve from an offline terrain knowledge system into a near real-time decision-support framework as improved data sources become available.

2.10 Chapter Summary

The design philosophy of HELPSs is founded on the principle of understanding terrain before evaluating helicopters. By separating terrain knowledge generation from helicopter-specific evaluation, the system remains modular, scalable, and reusable across multiple helicopter platforms and operational scenarios.
HELPSs is designed as an explainable, pilot-centric, and locally deployable decision-support system, ensuring that recommendations are transparent, operationally meaningful, and generated without compromising organizational confidentiality. These design principles establish the foundation for the system architecture and implementation methodology presented in the subsequent chapters.


Chapter 3: Overall System Architecture

3.1 System Overview

HELPSs adopts a modular two-stage architecture that separates terrain understanding from helicopter-specific landing evaluation. This separation is one of the fundamental design principles of the proposed system, allowing terrain analysis to be performed independently of any helicopter platform while enabling the resulting terrain knowledge to be reused for different operational scenarios.

The overall architecture consists of two major processing pipelines: the Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline and the Helicopter Landing Recommendation Pipeline, interconnected through a centralized Terrain Knowledge Database. The Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline is responsible for analyzing raw geospatial data and constructing a comprehensive, helicopter-independent representation of the terrain. The Helicopter Landing Recommendation Pipeline subsequently utilizes this knowledge to evaluate terrain suitability based on helicopter-specific operational requirements and mission context.

By separating terrain analysis from operational evaluation, HELPSs avoids repeated geospatial computation for every helicopter platform while maintaining scalability, modularity, and adaptability. This architecture also enables the system to operate as a locally deployable decision-support framework, preserving organizational confidentiality while allowing future integration with improved datasets and analytical capabilities.

Chapter 4: Terrain Knowledge Generation
Module 1: Data Acquisition
4.1.1 Overview
The Data Acquisition module serves as the foundation of the Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline. Its primary objective is to collect all geospatial datasets required to understand and characterize a selected Area of Interest (AOI). Rather than relying on a single data source, HELPSs integrates multiple complementary datasets to capture the terrain's geometric, topographical, environmental, and infrastructural characteristics.
The acquired datasets include satellite imagery, Digital Elevation Models (DEM), and Geographic Information System (GIS) layers. Together, these datasets provide the necessary information to identify candidate terrain, analyze elevation and slope, characterize surface properties, determine accessibility, and generate operational zones for emergency landing assessment.
Since terrain characteristics generally change slowly, data acquisition is performed periodically or whenever a new geographical region is selected for analysis. The collected datasets form the input for all subsequent modules within the Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline.

4.1.2Inputs
The Data Acquisition module requires the following inputs:
Input Dataset
Purpose
Area of Interest (AOI)
Defines the geographical region to be analyzed.
Satellite Imagery
Provides high-resolution visual information for identifying terrain features, vegetation, water bodies, and obstacles.
Digital Elevation Model (DEM)
Represents terrain elevation required for slope computation and terrain geometry analysis.
GIS Datasets
Provide vector information such as roads, rivers, administrative boundaries, and other geographic features.

4.1.3 Methodology
The Data Acquisition module is implemented through a sequence of standardized stages to ensure that all geospatial datasets required for terrain analysis are collected, processed, and organized consistently. Each stage prepares the necessary inputs for the subsequent modules while maintaining spatial consistency across all datasets.

Step 1: Define the Area of Interest (AOI)
Objective: Define the geographical region for which terrain knowledge will be generated. The AOI establishes the common spatial boundary for all datasets used throughout the Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline.
Implementation
Select the geographical region to be analyzed. 
Represent the AOI using a rectangular bounding box defined by minimum and maximum latitude and longitude. 
Assign a unique AOI identifier. 
Store the corresponding Coordinate Reference System (CRS). 
Use the AOI as the reference boundary for acquiring all geospatial datasets. 
Engineering Decision: A rectangular bounding box is selected for Version 1 due to its simplicity, computational efficiency, and compatibility with most geospatial data providers. Support for polygon-based AOIs can be incorporated in future versions for more precise region selection.
Output

Bounding Box Coordinates 
CRS Information

Objective: Acquire high-resolution satellite imagery of the selected AOI for identifying terrain features such as open land, vegetation, water bodies, and man-made structures.
Implementation
Query the selected AOI from the satellite imagery provider. 
Retrieve the most recent cloud-free imagery. 
Clip the imagery to the AOI boundary. 
Store the processed imagery in GeoTIFF format. 
Record acquisition metadata for future reference. 
Engineering Decision: Sentinel-2 MSI is selected for Version 1 because it provides free access, global coverage, regular updates, and adequate spatial resolution for large-scale terrain analysis.

Objective: Remove regions where helicopter landing is prohibited due to operational, legal, or security restrictions.
Implementation

Identify intersections between Restricted Areas and Candidate Terrain. 
Remove restricted regions from further processing. 
Preserve only terrain that is legally and operationally permissible for analysis.

Military installations 
International border-sensitive regions 
Government-protected facilities 
Critical infrastructure

Engineering Decision: Restricted Areas are excluded during terrain generation rather than during helicopter recommendation. This prevents prohibited locations from entering the Terrain Knowledge Database and ensures all stored terrain knowledge complies with operational restrictions.

Merge multiple tiles if necessary. 
Clip the DEM to the AOI boundary.

Validate the completeness of each layer.

Standardize file formats and metadata.

Engineering Decision: A standardized directory structure improves maintainability, reproducibility, and simplifies data access for downstream modules.

Organized AOI Dataset Repository

Module 2: Candidate Terrain Identification
4.2.1 Overview
The Candidate Terrain Identification module is the first analytical stage of the Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline. Its objective is to identify physically continuous terrain regions that are suitable for further evaluation by eliminating areas occupied by permanent obstacles and unsuitable land features.

Rather than identifying helicopter landing locations, this module only determines whether a terrain region is available for further analysis. The generated candidate terrains serve as the fundamental building blocks for subsequent modules, where their geometric, environmental, and operational characteristics are extracted.

A Candidate Terrain is therefore defined as:
A contiguous region that remains after eliminating permanent obstacles and unsuitable terrain features from the Area of Interest. It represents terrain that is physically available for further evaluation but has not yet been assessed for helicopter operational suitability.
The output of this module is a collection of Terrain Objects, each represented by its spatial boundary and unique identifier, which are forwarded to the Terrain Characterization module.

4.2.2 Inputs
The Candidate Terrain Identification module requires the standardized datasets generated by the Data Acquisition module.

Input
Purpose
Satellite Imagery
Identify terrain features and visible obstacles.
Digital Elevation Model (DEM)
Assist in distinguishing terrain structures and elevation characteristics.
GIS Layers
Detect roads, water bodies, buildings, and other mapped infrastructure.
AOI Metadata
Define the geographical boundary for analysis.


4.2.3 Methodology
The Candidate Terrain Identification module transforms raw geospatial information into Terrain Objects by progressively eliminating unsuitable regions and grouping the remaining terrain into contiguous candidate areas. Rather than directly searching for helicopter landing locations, this module identifies terrain that is physically available for further evaluation, while postponing operational suitability analysis to subsequent modules.
The methodology consists of the following stages:

Step 1: Terrain Classification
Objective: Classify every region within the Area of Interest into meaningful terrain categories that describe the physical nature of the land.
Implementation
Process the satellite imagery covering the AOI. 
Classify terrain into predefined land-cover categories. 
Generate a terrain classification map where each pixel belongs to a specific terrain class. 
Typical terrain classes include:
Grassland 
Bare Soil 
Agricultural Land 
Sparse Vegetation 
Dense Vegetation / Forest 
Water Bodies 
Urban / Built-up Areas 
Rocky Terrain 
Engineering Decision: The system first determines what each region represents before deciding whether it should be considered. Separating terrain classification from obstacle removal creates a modular pipeline and allows future support for additional terrain categories without modifying downstream modules.
Output: A classified terrain map of the selected AOI.


Step 2: Permanent Obstacle Identification
Objective: Identify terrain regions that are permanently unsuitable for helicopter landing.
Implementation
Using the classified terrain map together with GIS information, identify regions occupied by:
Buildings 
Dense forests 
Mountains / Cliff faces 
Large water bodies 
Urban infrastructure 
Other permanent obstacles 
These regions are marked as non-candidate terrain and excluded from further processing.
Engineering Decision: Only permanent obstacles are removed during this stage. Dynamic objects such as vehicles, temporary structures, or weather-related obstacles are intentionally excluded since they are outside the scope of Version 1.
Output: Obstacle mask representing permanently unsuitable terrain.

Step 3: Candidate Terrain Extraction
Objective: Extract terrain regions that remain after removing permanent obstacles.
Implementation
Remove obstacle pixels using the generated obstacle mask. 
Preserve only terrain classes that remain physically available. 
Ignore helicopter-specific requirements during this stage. 
Generate a binary candidate terrain map. 
Engineering Decision
HELPSs intentionally avoids applying helicopter constraints during terrain extraction. This ensures that the generated terrain knowledge remains helicopter-independent and reusable across multiple aircraft platforms.
Output: Binary Candidate Terrain Map.

Step 4: Connected Terrain Formation
Objective: Identify continuous terrain regions by grouping neighboring candidate pixels into individual terrain objects.
Implementation
Perform connected component analysis on the binary terrain map. 
Merge adjacent candidate pixels belonging to the same continuous land region. 
Assign a unique Terrain ID to every connected region. 
Disconnected regions are treated as independent Terrain Objects.
Engineering Decision: Terrain is represented as continuous connected regions instead of individual pixels. This significantly reduces computational complexity and establishes the fundamental spatial entity used throughout HELPSs.
Output: Connected Terrain Objects.

Step 5: Terrain Polygon Generation
Objective: Convert connected terrain regions into geospatial polygons.
Implementation
Extract the boundary of every connected terrain region. 
Generate polygon geometry representing the terrain extent. 
Store polygon coordinates together with the Terrain ID. 
These polygons provide the spatial representation used throughout subsequent modules.
Engineering Decision Polygon representation preserves the complete geometry of each terrain object while enabling efficient spatial queries and geometric analysis.
Output: Terrain Object Polygons.

Step 6: Terrain Object Storage
Objective: Store the extracted Terrain Objects within the Terrain Knowledge Database.
Implementation
For each Terrain Object, store:
Terrain ID 
Polygon Geometry 
Bounding Box 
AOI Reference 
Metadata 
Only the geometric representation is stored at this stage. Terrain characteristics and operational properties are extracted in the following modules.
Engineering Decision: Separating terrain geometry from terrain characteristics keeps the database normalized and allows each subsequent module to incrementally enrich the Terrain Object without duplicating information.
Output: Terrain Object Repository.

Module 3: Terrain Characterization
This module is about describing every Terrain Object that we extracted.

The Slope Analysis & Operational Zone Generation module transforms each Terrain Object into one or more Operational Zones by analyzing terrain slope at multiple thresholds. Each generated Operational Zone is stored independently together with its geometric properties, allowing the same Terrain Object to support multiple helicopter platforms and mission scenarios.

The Helicopter Landing Recommendation Pipeline utilizes the precomputed Terrain Knowledge Database to identify and recommend suitable emergency landing locations for a specific helicopter. Unlike the Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline, which performs computationally intensive terrain analysis, this pipeline focuses on helicopter-specific evaluation by considering the aircraft's operational limitations, mission requirements, and prevailing environmental conditions.
Rather than processing raw satellite imagery or Digital Elevation Models (DEM), the system retrieves precomputed Operational Zones and progressively evaluates them through a sequence of operational filters and ranking mechanisms. Chapter 7: Design Decisions and Engineering Components
7.1 Terrain Representation
One of the fundamental design decisions in HELPSs is representing the terrain as Terrain Objects instead of processing raw satellite imagery or Digital Elevation Models during every recommendation request. Each Terrain Object represents a physically continuous candidate terrain identified during the Terrain Knowledge Generation Pipeline and serves as the primary spatial entity within the Terrain Knowledge Database.
Terrain Objects are stored as polygon geometries, enabling efficient spatial queries, geometric analysis, and database storage. This representation eliminates repeated terrain processing, improves computational efficiency, and allows the same terrain knowledge to be reused across multiple helicopter platforms and mission scenarios.

Engineering Decision: Representing terrain as reusable polygon-based Terrain Objects separates terrain knowledge generation from helicopter recommendation, resulting in a modular, scalable, and computationally efficient system.

7.2 Connected Terrain Extraction
Identifying isolated land pixels is insufficient for helicopter landing assessment, as helicopters require continuous usable terrain rather than scattered land patches. Therefore, HELPSs groups adjacent candidate terrain pixels into connected regions, where each region represents a single Terrain Object.
This approach preserves the actual spatial continuity of the terrain, enables accurate geometric analysis, and forms the foundation for subsequent processes such as terrain characterization, slope analysis, and operational zone generation.

Engineering Decision: HELPSs represents terrain as connected regions instead of individual pixels to ensure that every Terrain Object corresponds to a physically continuous land area suitable for further operational evaluation. This significantly reduces computational complexity while providing a meaningful representation of the terrain.

7.3 Multi-Threshold Slope Representation
Traditional terrain analysis often represents an entire region using a single average slope, which can overlook significant local variations within the terrain. HELPSs instead adopts a multi-threshold slope representation, where each Terrain Object is analyzed across multiple slope thresholds (e.g., ≤0°, ≤2°, ≤4°, ≤6°, ≤8°, ≤10°, ≤12°, and ≤15°). This preserves the internal slope distribution and identifies all operationally feasible regions within the terrain.
By storing Operational Zones corresponding to each slope threshold, the same terrain knowledge can support helicopters with different allowable landing slope limits without recomputing terrain characteristics. This enables efficient helicopter-specific evaluation while maintaining a single reusable Terrain Knowledge Database.
Engineering Decision: HELPSs stores multiple slope-based Operational Zones rather than a single terrain slope value. This preserves terrain variability, supports multiple helicopter platforms, and eliminates repeated slope analysis during the recommendation phase.

7.4 Operational Zone Representation
A single Terrain Object may contain multiple regions with different slope characteristics, making the entire terrain unsuitable to be represented by a single landing location. HELPSs therefore divides each Terrain Object into one or more Operational Zones, where each zone represents a contiguous region satisfying a specific slope threshold.
Operational Zones are stored independently while maintaining their relationship with the parent Terrain Object. This hierarchical representation preserves terrain variability, avoids redundant terrain analysis, and enables efficient helicopter-specific retrieval during the recommendation process.

Engineering Decision: HELPSs adopts a hierarchical Terrain Object–Operational Zone model instead of representing each terrain with a single landing point. This allows multiple helicopters with different operational capabilities to evaluate the same terrain independently while maintaining a normalized and scalable Terrain Knowledge Database.

7.5 Landing Reference Point Selection
Selecting the geometric centroid of an Operational Zone does not always provide a safe landing location, particularly for irregularly shaped regions where the centroid may lie close to the boundary or even outside the usable area. To overcome this limitation, HELPSs uses the Pole of Inaccessibility as the representative landing point, as it corresponds to the location farthest from all surrounding boundaries.
This point also provides the Maximum Inscribed Circle Radius, representing the largest circular landing footprint that can fit entirely within the Operational Zone. Together, these measures improve landing safety and simplify subsequent landing footprint validation.

Engineering Decision: HELPSs adopts the Pole of Inaccessibility instead of the centroid because it maximizes boundary clearance and provides a safer, more reliable reference point for helicopter landing evaluation.

7.6 Landing Footprint Validation
A location with sufficient area is not necessarily suitable for helicopter landing. The helicopter's landing footprint, including the required safety clearance around the aircraft, must fit completely within the Operational Zone without intersecting its boundaries or internal exclusion regions.
HELPSs validates the landing footprint by placing the helicopter's required clearance circle at the Pole of Inaccessibility and verifying that it remains entirely within the usable Operational Zone. This geometric validation ensures that every recommended landing location can physically accommodate the helicopter while maintaining the prescribed safety margins.

Engineering Decision: HELPSs performs geometric landing footprint validation after operational constraint matching to ensure that only physically feasible landing locations are recommended, improving both operational safety and recommendation reliability.

7.7 Environmental Context Representation
Terrain geometry alone is insufficient to determine the operational suitability of a landing location. Factors such as surrounding obstacles, accessibility, nearby water bodies, and available approach space significantly influence the practicality and safety of helicopter operations. HELPSs therefore associates each Operational Zone with an Environmental Context, providing additional information about its surroundings without modifying the underlying terrain representation.
This separation enables environmental attributes to influence recommendation quality while preserving the helicopter-independent nature of the Terrain Knowledge Database.

Engineering Decision: HELPSs stores environmental information as a separate contextual layer rather than embedding it within terrain geometry. This improves modularity, simplifies future extensions, and enables different recommendation strategies without modifying the terrain representation.

7.8 Configurable Recommendation Framework
Different emergency scenarios require different landing priorities. For example, a medical evacuation may prioritize accessibility, whereas an engine failure may prioritize the safest immediately reachable landing location. To accommodate these varying operational requirements, HELPSs employs a configurable recommendation framework instead of a fixed scoring model.
The final suitability score is computed by combining Static Terrain Features, Dynamic Weather Conditions, and Mission Context, allowing the recommendation strategy to adapt without altering the underlying Terrain Knowledge Database.

Engineering Decision: HELPSs separates the recommendation strategy from terrain knowledge generation by adopting a configurable scoring framework. This enables future integration of alternative ranking techniques, mission-specific priorities, and optimization algorithms without requiring architectural changes.

7.9 Separation of Knowledge Generation and Recommendation
One of the key architectural decisions in HELPSs is the separation of Terrain Knowledge Generation from the Helicopter Landing Recommendation Pipeline. Terrain analysis is performed once to generate reusable terrain knowledge, which is stored within the Terrain Knowledge Database. During recommendation, the system queries this precomputed knowledge instead of repeating computationally expensive terrain analysis.
This separation improves computational efficiency, reduces response time, supports multiple helicopter platforms, and enables the Terrain Knowledge Database to be updated independently of the recommendation engine.

Engineering Decision: HELPSs adopts a two-stage architecture consisting of offline terrain knowledge generation and online helicopter recommendation. This modular design improves scalability, reusability, maintainability, and overall system performance.

7.10 Chapter Summary
The engineering decisions presented in this chapter define the core architectural principles of HELPSs. Concepts such as Terrain Objects, Operational Zones, multi-threshold slope representation, Pole of Inaccessibility, environmental context modeling, and the separation of terrain knowledge generation from recommendation collectively improve the system's modularity, computational efficiency, scalability, and adaptability. These design choices establish a reusable terrain knowledge framework capable of supporting diverse helicopter platforms, mission profiles, and future enhancements without requiring fundamental architectural changes.


Chapter 8: System Development Roadmap
8.1 Development Philosophy
The development of HELPSs follows a modular and incremental approach, where the system is constructed through a sequence of independent yet interconnected phases. Each phase produces a well-defined deliverable that serves as the foundation for the subsequent phase, enabling progressive system development, easier debugging, and independent validation of individual components.
Rather than building the complete system as a single application, HELPSs separates terrain knowledge generation from helicopter recommendation, allowing computationally intensive terrain analysis to be performed once and reused across multiple recommendation requests. This phased development strategy improves maintainability, scalability, and simplifies future system enhancements.
