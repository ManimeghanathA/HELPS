Entire pipeline from the satellite to the final land detection:
Step 1: What does the satellite actually see?
- It captures an image like a camera but the satellite records much more than the normal image.
For every image it also stores

Satellite position (GPS)
Satellite orientation (roll, pitch, yaw)
Camera focal length
Pixel size of the sensor
Time of capture
Viewing angle

This is called the sensor model.

Step 2: Why is Raw image wrong 
With Change in the position and angle of the satellite image, the object under the image get distorted - This is called relief displacement
This problem is solved by Orthorectification

Step 3: Orthorectification:
Orthorectification removes the distortions and answers where the point is actually located on earth
we pixel(500,500) along with this we need Latitude, Longitude, Elevation
For the above things we need, Camera Geometry, Satellite position, terrain height (Comes from DEM)

DEM: Digital Elevation model - Its a division of a location into a grid which holds a number stating the height above the sea level

DSM: Digital Surface model

Limitations:
difficult or impossible to detect reliably:
Thin power lines
Temporary vehicles
People
Loose debris
Recently erected structures
Ground firmness (e.g., mud beneath dry-looking soil)
Weather conditions at landing time
